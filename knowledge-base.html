Getting Started
New to Brightfield? Start here.
What exactly is Launch Operations Consulting? +
Launch operations consulting means getting your business back end built properly from the start. Most business consultants focus on strategy — your market, your pricing, your growth plan. That's valuable, but it doesn't tell you who follows up with the client, where the contract lives, how your new hire knows what to do on day one, or what happens when you're sick and the business still needs to run.

That's what launch operations consulting addresses. It's the systems, workflows, tools, and processes that make a business actually function. At Brightfield, we work specifically with new and early-stage businesses because the first year is when these foundations are either built or skipped. Most founders skip them because they're busy — and spend the next few years paying for it.

The difference from general consulting is that we don't hand you a strategy deck and leave. We get into the details, build the actual systems, and hand over something your business can run on.
Is Brightfield right for my business? +
Brightfield is built for founders who are excellent at what they do — their craft, their service, their product — but who didn't sign up to be an operations manager. If you're in your first year of business and things feel a bit disorganised in the background, you're probably the right fit.

More specifically, Brightfield works best with:
— New or pre-launch businesses that want to start with the right structure in place
— Newly launched businesses (up to 12 months in) that are running but feel disorganised
— Founders who are doing everything themselves and need systems so the business doesn't depend entirely on them
— Small teams where onboarding, communication, and task management are inconsistent

If you have a large, established team or you're looking for high-level strategic advisory, Brightfield may not be the right fit. But if your business is early-stage and the back end needs to be built — that's exactly what we do.

If it's a fit, we'll follow up with a custom proposal. If it's not, we'll tell you honestly.

Operations & Systems
Understanding the backbone of a well-run business.
What is a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) and why does my business need one? +
An SOP is a written record of how something gets done in your business. That's it. It's the answer to "How do we handle this?" — written down somewhere accessible so the answer isn't just in your head.

Think of it this way: the first time you onboard a new client, you figure it out as you go. The second time, you remember most of it. By the tenth time, you've got a rhythm — but it only exists in your memory. An SOP takes that rhythm and puts it on paper (or a doc, or a workflow tool) so that:

— You're not reinventing the process every single time
— Someone else can do it without asking you ten questions
— Nothing important gets skipped when you're busy or overwhelmed
— Your business can run even when you're not the one running it

SOPs don't have to be complicated. A numbered list of steps is enough to start. The goal isn't perfection — it's consistency. When things are written down, your business stops depending entirely on you remembering everything and starts operating like an actual organisation.

At Brightfield, building your first SOPs is part of what we do from day one, because documented processes are what separate a sustainable business from a founder burning out trying to hold everything together.
What tools does Brightfield recommend for new businesses? +
You don't need an expensive tech stack to run a well-organised business. Here are the tools we typically recommend for new businesses, by category — all beginner-friendly and free or low-cost to start.

CRM tools:
— HubSpot — robust free tier, great for small teams just getting started
— Notion — not a traditional CRM, but works well as a lightweight client tracker in the early stages

Project management tools:
— Trello — simple, visual, easy to learn; great for solopreneurs and small teams
— Notion — highly flexible; can double as your SOP library and project tracker
— Asana — slightly more structured; good if you're managing multiple clients or projects

Communication tools:
— Slack — free tier works well for small teams; keeps work conversations off your personal phone
— Gmail (Google Workspace) — professional email with shared drives, calendars, and docs built in

Email Marketing & Client Communication:
— Mailchimp — free up to 500 contacts; good for newsletters and client updates
— Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) — generous free tier, simple to set up

The honest advice: pick one tool per category and actually use it. The best tool is the one your team opens every day — not the most feature-rich one that nobody touches.
What does a proper customer onboarding process look like? +
Customer onboarding is what happens between "they said yes" and "they're a happy, informed client." Done well, it sets the tone for the entire relationship. Done poorly — or not at all — it's where trust starts to erode before you've even delivered anything.

Here's what a solid onboarding flow looks like:

1. Welcome — As soon as the client signs, they should hear from you. A welcome email (or message) that confirms the engagement, expresses genuine enthusiasm, and tells them exactly what happens next. No one should be left wondering, "So… what now?"

2. Expectation Setting — Before work begins, make sure the client knows what you'll deliver, when they'll hear from you, how to reach you, and what you need from them. This is where scope creep and miscommunication get prevented before they start.

3. First Touchpoint — The first real interaction after onboarding sets the tone. Whether it's a kickoff call, a questionnaire, or a shared document — make it structured, make it easy for them, and make it clear you're organised and in control.

4. Check-ins — Regular, scheduled check-ins keep the relationship healthy and catch problems early. They don't have to be long. A quick update email or a 15-minute call every week or two goes a long way toward making clients feel valued and informed.

5. Completion & Offboarding — When the engagement ends, don't just disappear. A proper offboarding includes a summary of what was delivered, any handover documents, a space for feedback, and a warm close that leaves the door open for future work or referrals.

A good onboarding process doesn't just make clients feel good — it reduces back-and-forth, sets boundaries early, and gives you a repeatable system so every client gets the same quality experience regardless of how busy you are.
How do I know if my operations are broken? +
Operations tend to break quietly. There's rarely one dramatic moment — it's usually a slow accumulation of small things that eventually cost you clients, revenue, or your own sanity. Here are five signs it's time to pay attention:

1. Follow-ups are falling through the cracks — Leads go cold because nobody follows up. Clients don't hear from you for two weeks. Emails go unanswered. If keeping up with communication feels like a constant battle, the problem isn't effort — it's the lack of a system.

2. Your team doesn't know what they're supposed to be doing — New hires ask the same questions repeatedly. Tasks get duplicated or dropped. People are unclear on their roles. When your team can't operate without checking with you first, that's an operations problem.

3. Nothing is written down — If the answer to "how do we do this?" is always "ask me," your business is entirely dependent on your memory. One busy week, one sick day, one new hire — and things start to slip.

4. Clients are complaining about the experience — Not your product or service — the experience. Late responses, unclear communication, inconsistent delivery, feeling like they have to chase you. These are signs the client journey hasn't been designed — just improvised.

5. You're doing everything yourself, and still things are getting missed — If you're the last line of defence on every task, every follow-up, and every decision, that's not hustle — that's a structural problem. A business that only works when the founder is doing everything isn't scalable, and it's not sustainable.

If two or more of these sound familiar, it's worth having a conversation. That's exactly what the discovery call is for.
Our Packages
Understanding what's included and what's right for you.
What's the difference between The Launch Blueprint and The First 90 Days? +
Both packages are built for new businesses, but they serve different needs depending on how much support you're looking for.

The Launch Blueprint is a one-time setup delivered in two weeks. Think of it as getting your operational foundation built quickly and correctly: your tool stack configured, your core communication templates ready, your first SOP documented, and a clear blueprint for how your business should run. After delivery, you take it from there. It's ideal if you're confident running day-to-day operations yourself and just need the structure handed to you.

The First 90 Days is everything in The Launch Blueprint, plus three months of active, hands-on support. We don't just hand you a system and step back — we work alongside you while your business is actually running. That means managing customer communications, building SOPs in real time as situations arise, supporting your first hires, and checking in weekly so nothing slips through. It's designed for founders who want operational support while they focus on delivering their product or service.

The simplest way to think about it: The Launch Blueprint gives you the tools and the plan. The First 90 Days gives you the tools, the plan, and someone in the back end making sure it all runs while you find your footing.
Who is The Foundation Audit for? +
The Foundation Audit is for businesses that are already running but feel like they're operating on duct tape.

If you launched six to twelve months ago and things are moving but chaotically, this package is designed for you. Maybe you've been too busy to build proper systems. Maybe you brought on a team member and the onboarding was messy. Maybe clients are having inconsistent experiences and you're not sure why. Maybe you just have a nagging sense that the back end of your business isn't where it needs to be — even if you can't put your finger on exactly what's wrong.

The audit gives you a clear picture of where things stand: a full review of your current operations, a gap analysis that identifies what's missing or broken, and a prioritised action plan so you know exactly what to fix and in what order. It also includes a follow-up session 30 days later to see how implementation is going.

It's not about starting over. It's about understanding what you've built, what's working, what isn't, and what needs to change — with a concrete plan to get there.

Working With Us
What to expect when you become a Brightfield client.
How do we communicate during the engagement? +
Communication during the engagement happens via email and WhatsApp — whichever you prefer, or a combination of both. We'll align on that at the start, so there's no confusion about where to reach us.

Weekly check-ins are the backbone of the working relationship. At each check-in, we'll cover what's been done, what's coming next, and anything that needs your input — and we'll send across a progress report so you always have a written record of where things stand. No need to rely on memory from the last conversation.

Between check-ins, if something urgent comes up that can't wait until the next scheduled session, just send a message or email to flag it. We're not unreachable between calls. If it matters, reach out.

The goal is simple: you should never feel like you're in the dark about what's happening in your own business. We keep communication consistent, clear, and documented — because that's exactly the standard we'd be holding your operations to as well.
Will I own everything that's built during our engagement? +
Completely and without exception. Every SOP, template, workflow, communication script, onboarding document, and system we build during your engagement belongs to you.

This is a core part of how Brightfield operates. The goal from day one is to build things you can own, run, and hand off to your team — not to create a situation where your business only works while we're involved. That would defeat the entire purpose.

When the engagement ends, you'll have a fully documented, operational back end that your team can use without us. No proprietary software that locks you in. No processes that only make sense to us. Everything is built to be understandable, transferable, and yours.

We're not in the business of making ourselves indispensable. We're in the business of making your business run properly — and then stepping back.
What do I need to prepare before we start? +
Not as much as you might think. You don't need to have everything figured out before we begin — that's partly why you're here. But there are a few things that will help us hit the ground running:

Access to your tools — If you're already using a CRM, project management tool, email platform, or any other business software, we'll need access from the start. If you don't have any tools set up yet, that's fine. Setting up your stack is often one of the first things we do together.

A clear sense of your business goals — You don't need a polished business plan, but it helps to know what you're building toward. Who are your clients? What does a good first year look like for you? What does success feel like six months from now? The more context you can give us, the more precisely we can build systems that actually serve your vision.

Time to be involved — We do the heavy lifting, but this isn't a fully hands-off process. You'll need to show up for weekly check-ins, respond to questions when they come up, and review and approve what gets built. The businesses that get the most out of working with Brightfield are the ones where the founder stays engaged even when things get busy.

If you're not sure whether you're ready, the discovery call is the right place to start. We'll tell you honestly what we'd need and whether the timing makes sense.
What if I'm not satisfied with the outcome? +
We take this seriously — and we'd rather address it directly than hide behind vague reassurances.

First, the way we work is designed to prevent dissatisfaction from building quietly. Weekly check-ins with progress reports and an open feedback loop mean that if something isn't landing right — a deliverable isn't what you expected, or a system doesn't fit how your business actually works — we hear about it early and adjust. Most issues get resolved before they become real problems.

If a deliverable doesn't meet the agreed scope, we revise it. We're not in the habit of delivering something, calling it done, and moving on. We stay with it until it's right.

And if, at the end of an engagement, you feel that expectations weren't met, we'll have an honest conversation about what happened and what we can do about it. That might mean additional revisions, an extended session, or revisiting a specific deliverable. We don't have a rigid policy for every scenario because every situation is different — but the commitment is consistent: we stand behind our work, and we won't leave a client worse off than when they came to us.

The best protection against a bad outcome, though, is the work we do upfront: the discovery call, the custom proposal, and the clear scope we agree on before anything starts. That alignment is what sets the rest of the engagement up to go well.
